**Stated by Gil 2026-10-05 (as a note "on your end", while running /remote-control):** the end product of the cluster on the AI side is a **self-training video & mood processor**. Mood is perceived from **BPM and pitch/tempo cues** and/or **video mood feedback from a crowd** (if a crowd exists).

Purpose: **autonomous Resolume control driven by music input**. Accepted sources: DJ, Aux, Apple Music, anything routed into VoiceMeeter, or external input. Includes an **optional handoff to a human operator through OSC and an interaction table** (human can take over/steer, then hand back).

**Why:** this is the north star the AV pieces are building toward, so design choices (what gets logged, what is controllable, how overrides work) should serve it rather than only the individual tools.

**How to apply:**
- Treat existing pieces as stages of this pipeline: music capture + BPM/levels (`magi-audio` on repositorium, VBAN from VoiceMeeter, see [[magi-av-mixer-idea]]), OSC back into Arena (tempo already verified/enabled there), NDI video feeds + recording ([[magi-nvenc-recorder]]), crowd/video perception on the Hailo NPUs ([[magi-local-llm-feasibility]], [[magi-custom-model-goal]]), routing/LLM layer ([[magi-router]]).
- "Self-training" implies logging (audio features + Resolume state/actions + any crowd feedback) into the existing Postgres `events` / Redis streams so a policy can later be learned from it; keep that data capture in mind when extending services.
- Human handoff must be a first-class mode: OSC control surface + an interaction table (who/what controls each parameter, auto vs manual), with a safe way to hand control back and forth. Never let autonomous control fight a human who has taken over.
- Not yet designed or built; nothing from this note has been implemented. Open questions to ask Gil when starting: which Resolume parameters the AI may drive (clip triggers, effects, opacity, tempo), how crowd mood is observed (cameras? which), and what the interaction table looks like (physical controller vs web page).
